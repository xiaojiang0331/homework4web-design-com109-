THE PHOTOGRAPHERS

Six lives.
Lasting impressions.

Meet six influential figures in the history of photography. Open each story to explore their approach and find one idea to try yourself.

Landscape · United States · 1902–1984

Ansel Adams

Mountain light. Deep shadows. A landscape made unforgettable.

Adams is known for his carefully composed black-and-white photographs of the American West. His landscapes invite us to notice the relationship between light, texture and the natural world.

How to look

Look for a wide range of tones, from deep black shadows to bright highlights. Notice how the foreground leads your eye towards the mountains.

Try this yourself

Try placing a dark foreground in front of a brighter background. A strong photograph needs structure as well as a beautiful subject.

Documentary · United States · 1895–1965

Dorothea Lange

Human stories, told with attention and empathy.

Lange documented people affected by the Great Depression. Her photograph Migrant Mother, made in Nipomo, California, became one of the best-known images of that period.

How to look

Notice the close framing and the arrangement of faces and hands. The picture directs attention towards the mother’s expression.

Try this yourself

Before taking a portrait, consider the person and their circumstances. Context is part of the story, not an optional extra.

Street · France · 1857–1927

Eugène Atget

The quiet poetry of an ordinary street.

Atget recorded the buildings, streets and details of Paris. His photographs preserve the character of a changing city and encourage us to look carefully at everyday places.

How to look

Study the lines of the buildings and the space between them. Architectural details become the main subjects of the image.

Try this yourself

Return to a familiar street with a camera. Look for details you usually walk past, such as a doorway, shopfront or pattern of shadows.

Portrait · United Kingdom · 1815–1879

Julia Margaret Cameron

A face becomes a study of character and light.

Cameron was a pioneering nineteenth-century portrait photographer. Her expressive portraits explored photography as an art form rather than simply a way to record a likeness.

How to look

Notice the soft focus, directional light and close framing. The portrait gives more attention to expression than to a detailed background.

Try this yourself

Use soft light from a window and a simple background. Watch how light changes the mood of a face.

Documentary · United States · 1874–1940

Lewis Hine

A camera used to make working lives visible.

Hine used photography to document social conditions, including child labour. His pictures for the National Child Labor Committee made the circumstances of young workers visible to a wider public.

How to look

Look at the relationship between the child and the machinery. The surroundings communicate as much as the portrait itself.

Try this yourself

Include enough of the environment to explain what is happening. A photograph can show a person and the conditions around them.

Documentary · United States · 1912–2006

Gordon Parks

Everyday life, seen through a lens of dignity.

Parks worked across photography, writing and film. His early documentary work explored race, poverty and daily life. American Gothic portrays the government cleaning worker Ella Watson.

How to look

Observe the flag, the cleaning tools and Watson’s direct presence. Familiar objects help carry the photograph’s meaning.

Try this yourself

Think about the objects around your subject. A setting can add meaning without requiring a complicated composition.
